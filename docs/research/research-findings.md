# Win the Jira Exit, Then Rebuild Everything

Remaking Jira as an open-source, self-hostable product with every premium and enterprise feature included is feasible, and the research puts its size at roughly **700,000 lines of application code**, equal to about **230 person-years** of human effort, because "Jira" in 2026 is really seven products (Jira Software, the JSM-based Service Collection with Opsgenie and Assets, business spaces, Product Discovery, Align, Automation and Rovo AI) plus a Marketplace whose best-selling apps fill gaps Jira never closed. The right sequence is exit first, breadth second: first a "Data Center exit" core of about 100,000 lines (Jira Software parity, a JQL-compatible query engine, a Jira-shaped REST shim, a lossless DC and Cloud importer, and free SSO, audit, backup and one-command self-hosting), released before Atlassian's **30 March 2028** cutoff on DC expansions and well ahead of the **28 March 2029** read-only date ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life)). Automation, cross-team Plans, approvals, scripting and a service-desk core follow; then ITSM, on-call, status pages and a CMDB; then discovery, portfolio and test management. The opening is real. Open-source rivals gate what Jira refugees need most: Plane keeps custom properties and its Jira importer out of its AGPL edition, and OpenProject charges for SSO and SCIM ([Plane](https://plane.so/pricing); [Plane](https://docs.plane.so/importers/jira); [OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/pricing/)). The research found no open project with a JQL-grade query language, and Atlassian starts metering automation steps and AI credits on top of seat prices on **3 December 2026** ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/expanded-usage-based-pricing)). The recommended build is a Postgres-only TypeScript modular monolith with typed custom-field storage, an in-house JQL-to-SQL compiler, OpenFGA authorization, a Linear-style change-log sync engine and WASM plugins, shipped as one container under AGPL-3.0. The legal limits come from Atlassian's contracts and trademarks rather than API copyright, so the project needs clean-room discipline and no "Jira" in its name.

*How to read this report: sentences carrying an inline link are cited evidence from the research notes. Statements tagged **[Estimate]** or **[Recommendation]** are this report's own analysis; every effort figure and technology choice falls in that category unless a link is attached.*

## Parity means rebuilding seven products and the apps that patch them

Jira Software alone carries a configuration surface that took Atlassian two decades to accrete. A site mixes company-managed spaces, where admins share schemes for work types, fields, screens, workflows, permissions, notifications and item security, with team-managed spaces whose configuration stays local and is capped at **50 custom fields** ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/what-are-team-managed-and-company-managed-projects/); [Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/available-custom-fields-for-team-managed-projects/)). The workflow engine ships **17 built-in conditions** with nested all/any grouping, about a dozen validators and five essential post functions that always run in a fixed order ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-cloud-administration/docs/configure-advanced-issue-workflows/)). JQL offers **18 operators**, about 60 searchable system fields and roughly 30 core functions plus about 20 service-desk functions, yet its history operators WAS and CHANGED work on only **six fields** and scan only the latest 10,000 changes per item ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/jql-operators/); [Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/jql-functions/)). Premium layers on Plans, a sandboxed, scenario-capable cross-team planner with capacity, dependencies and a program board, capped at **10,000 work items and 200 spaces** per plan ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/what-is-advanced-roadmaps/); [Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/limits-on-plan-size-in-advanced-roadmaps/)), and since March 2026 Atlassian has enforced **700 fields and 150 work types per space**, with eight more limits rolling out from September ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-cloud-administration/docs/data-limits-and-guardrails/)).

Atlassian is simplifying its own model, which tells a rebuild where to aim. Field configurations and their schemes are being folded into a single "field scheme" ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-cloud-administration/docs/what-are-field-configurations-and-field-configuration-schemes/)), the new workflow editor publishes instantly and edits active workflows in place ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-cloud-administration/docs/what-is-the-new-workflow-editor/)), and the docs now call projects "spaces" and issues "work items" while warning that queries written with the new terms do not all work yet ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/jql-functions/)). **[Recommendation]** A remake should implement the simplified target model natively, map the legacy scheme stack only inside its importer, and accept both the old and the new JQL vocabulary.

Everything beyond Jira Software is a separate product with its own paywall. Since 7 October 2025 JSM has been sold only inside the **Service Collection** alongside Customer Service Management, Assets and Rovo ([Seibert](https://us.seibert.group/blog/atlassian-service-collection)), and its Premium tier holds change, problem and major-incident management, voice alerts, heartbeats, AIOps and a virtual agent ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/jsm-cloud-pricing-packaging-update); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/service-desk/premium)). Opsgenie's alerting and on-call were rebuilt inside JSM ahead of Opsgenie's **5 April 2027** end of support ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-service-management-cloud/docs/should-i-merge-opsgenie-with-jira-service-management/)). Assets adds a CMDB with its own query language, discovery and reconciliation, metered with object allowances of **5,000, 50,000 and 500,000** by tier ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/service-collection); [Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/assets/docs/set-up-assets-data-manager/)), while Statuspage is still priced separately at **$29 to $1,499 a month** ([Hyperping](https://hyperping.com/blog/statuspage-pricing)). Product Discovery charges **$10 or $25 per creator** and still lacks a public feedback portal ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/product-discovery/pricing); [ProductLift](https://www.productlift.dev/compare/jira-product-discovery-alternatives/)); Jira Align sits in a Strategy Collection reportedly priced at **$77,400 a year for 1–50 users** ([ONES](https://ones.com/blog/?p=1054814)); and Compass reached end of sale on 13 May 2026, with its catalog moving to the acquired DX ([Adaptavist](https://www.adaptavist.com/blog/what-you-need-to-know-about-atlassian-compass-end-of-sale); [Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/compass/docs/announce-component-changes/)). Automation offers roughly 60 triggers but caps each flow at **65 steps** and each scheduled JQL batch at **999 items** ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/cloud-automation/docs/jira-automation-triggers/); [Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/cloud-automation/docs/automation-service-limits/)), and Rovo layers AI chat, agents, coding agents and an MCP server on top ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-software-cloud/docs/work-with-ai-agents-in-jira/); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-rovo-mcp-ga)).

The Marketplace defines the rest of the parity target. It lists **8,000+ apps** with an estimated **$1.8B in 2024 sales**, a third-party figure ([Aventis Advisors](http://aventis-advisors.com/atlassian-marketplace-ma/)), and its highest-install paid Jira apps — ScriptRunner at about 34,000–37,000 installs, Tempo Timesheets at 26,000–31,000 and Xray at 25,000–29,000, followed by JMWE, Structure, BigPicture and eazyBI ([Atlassian Marketplace](https://marketplace.atlassian.com/addons/app/jira/top-selling)) — map the jobs Jira never absorbed: scripting, time and cost, test management, cross-project hierarchy and BI. Workflow conditions, validators and post functions are still in Preview on Forge in 2026 ([Atlassian](https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/forge/manifest-reference/modules/index-jira/)), which is why workflow-extension apps keep selling. **[Estimate]** "Every premium feature" therefore means Jira's own tiers plus roughly ten Marketplace categories.

The price stack shows what an all-included open product displaces. At list price, a team of up to 100 that needs Premium features plus enforced SSO pays **$18.30 + $4.20 = $22.50 per user per month** for Jira Premium and Atlassian Guard Standard ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/pricing); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/guard/pricing)), and a third party reports Premium rising to $19.60 on 13 October 2026 ([Breeze](https://www.breeze.pm/articles/atlassian-pricing-changes-2026)). From **3 December 2026**, automation beyond 400, 750 or 1,000 steps per user per month costs **$0.50 per 1,000 steps** and AI beyond the credit allowance costs **$0.01 per credit**, with extra usage switched on by default ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/cloud-automation/docs/how-is-my-usage-calculated/); [Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/organization-administration/docs/usage-limits-in-atlassian-intelligence/)); Enterprise automation went from unlimited to 1,000 steps per user in the same change ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/atlassian.jira/diffs/2026W37)). Third parties quote JSM Premium at **$51.42–$57.30 per agent per month** ([Unthread](https://unthread.io/blog/jira-service-management-pricing/); [Macha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/jira-service-management-pricing-explained)).

## Atlassian's own deadlines open a 2027–2029 window for self-hosters

Atlassian stopped selling Data Center to new customers on **30 March 2026**, stops expansions for existing customers on **30 March 2028**, and turns Jira, JSM and Confluence DC **read-only on 28 March 2029**, with extended maintenance available only by exception ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life)). Its claim that 99% of customers are in or on a path to the cloud, including 75% of regulated and enterprise customers ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life)), implies **[Estimate]** that up to a quarter of regulated and enterprise customers had no cloud path at announcement. Their options are thin. Government Cloud holds only FedRAMP Moderate, granted in March 2025 ([Atlassian IR](https://investors.atlassian.com/news/news-details/2025/Atlassian-Achieves-FedRAMPR-Moderate-Authorization-for-Atlassian-Government-Cloud/default.aspx)); FedRAMP High and IL5 environments are promised before the DC end of life without control over authorization timing ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life)); single-tenant Isolated Cloud was still on a path to general availability in 2026 ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/expanding-pathways-to-cloud)); and HIPAA customers on Atlassian Cloud must switch AI off across the whole organization ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/organization-administration/docs/the-hipaa-implementation-guide)).

Costs and friction push in the same direction. DC prices rose 5–20% in February 2025 and Cloud prices 5–10% in October 2025, before the December 2026 meters arrive ([Atlassian](https://s206.q4cdn.com/270053503/files/doc_downloads/2025/03/FY25-pricing-and-packaging-changes.pdf); [Atlassian](https://s206.q4cdn.com/270053503/files/doc_downloads/2025/09/Fiscal-2026-Cloud-Pricing-notice.pdf)). Admins also live with API tokens that last at most one year with no central renewal ([Atlassian Community](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-questions/API-token-longer-than-1-year/qaa-p/3225875)), an audit log that reaches back at most **180 days** ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/security-and-access-policies/docs/view-audit-log-activities/)), and native backups that do not undo customer deletions, with Backup and Restore sold as an annual Premium and Enterprise add-on ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/trust/reliability/cloud-architecture-and-operational-practices); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/platform/infrastructure/backup/pricing)).

The surrounding ecosystem is being disrupted at the same moment. Connect apps lose support on **31 January 2027** ([Atlassian Developer Community](https://community.developer.atlassian.com/t/connect-end-of-support-extended-to-january-31-2027/102002)), Compass access ends on 31 December 2027 ([Cortex](https://www.cortex.io/post/atlassian-compass-end-of-life-what-to-do-next)), and every third-party DC Marketplace app licence expires with DC ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/data-center-end-of-life)). **[Estimate]** DC app vendors therefore lose their on-premises revenue line in 2029 and are natural recruits for a new self-hosted extension platform. Conversely, the Opsgenie exodus — end of support on 5 April 2027 — will be over before any project started in 2027 can ship on-call, so it is not a launch market.

User sentiment explains why the window matters beyond compliance. G2 reviews flag Jira's learning curve about **682** times, call it overwhelming about 504 times, report lag about **327** times and call it expensive about 179 times ([G2](https://g2.com/products/jira/reviews)). The Pragmatic Engineer's 2025 survey of 2,997 engineers found Jira the most-disliked tool, with slowness the top complaint and Linear the most-cited replacement ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pragmatic-engineer-2025-survey)). Linear passed **$100M ARR** in August 2026 ([Linear](https://linear.app/now)) and moved Oscar Health's 600+ people off a famously complex Jira instance in about a month ([Linear](https://linear.app/customers/oscar)). Yet Linear has no native time tracking and no per-state permissions, which a migration write-up flags as a problem for regulated industries ([Cotera](https://cotera.co/articles/linear-vs-jira-comparison)) — exactly the buyers the DC end of life strands.

The money behind the window is large. Project-management software is worth about **$7.24B (2025)** growing to $12.02B by 2030, and ITSM about **$9.41B (2025)** growing to $22.91B by 2031, by analyst estimates ([monday.com citing Mordor Intelligence](https://monday.com/blog/project-management/project-management-statistics/); [MarketsandMarkets via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15667682435793920)); JSM alone serves 65,000+ organizations ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260205386782/en/)). In Europe, OpenProject ships as the project-management component of Germany's openDesk and has joined the EuroCommons coalition ([OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/opendesk/); [OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/blog/eurocommons-openproject-xwiki/)). **[Estimate]** The window rewards a product that is generally available for Jira Software workloads by early 2028 and for service desks by early 2029.

## Open-source rivals gate exactly what Jira refugees need

Plane is the closest modern analog and the clearest illustration of the gap. It has **60.7k GitHub stars** and an AGPL Community Edition ([GitHub](https://github.com/makeplane/plane)), but its Commercial and Air-gapped editions are separate, closed codebases ([Plane](https://developers.plane.so/self-hosting/editions-and-versions)). Work item types, custom properties, worklogs, workflows and approvals, automations, and email or form intake sit in the paid Pro and Business tiers ([Plane](https://plane.so/pricing)). Its Jira importer — the most complete in the open-source field, carrying history, sprints, custom fields and worklogs — runs only on Plane Cloud and the Commercial edition ([Plane](https://docs.plane.so/importers/jira)), and since April 2026 new self-hosted purchases carry **10-seat (Commercial) and 100-seat (Air-gapped) minimums** ([Plane forum](https://forum.plane.so/t/new-seat-minimums-on-self-hosted-commercial-and-air-gapped-starting-april-24/96)). Plane admits that reversing its promise of Community Edition SSO in January 2024 cost it community trust ([Plane](https://plane.so/blog/lessons-in-sustainability-from-scaling-in-open-source)), and its long-running GitHub discussion collects accusations that its open-source label is misleading ([GitHub](https://github.com/orgs/makeplane/discussions/1266)).

OpenProject is the depth benchmark, with Gantt baselines, time and cost tracking, budgets, meetings, a wiki and, since 17.0, real-time collaborative documents in every edition ([OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/pricing/); [OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/docs/release-notes/17-0-0/)), plus the deepest contributor base at **413** ([GitHub](https://github.com/opf/openproject)). Its gaps fall where Jira migrants hurt most: SAML/OIDC SSO, LDAP group sync, SCIM, the team planner, portfolio management and its MCP server are Enterprise add-ons ([OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/pricing/)); its Jira Migrator is a beta that supports only Jira Server/DC 10.x–11.x ([OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/docs/installation-and-operations/jira-migration/)); and its Redmine-inherited custom fields are system-wide rather than per-project, which the team itself documents as a mapping problem ([OpenProject](https://openproject.org/blog/jira-migrator-custom-fields)). It does publish the category's most rigorous sizing guidance, from 4 cores and 4 GB for 200 users up to an 80–100k-user reference configuration ([OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/docs/installation-and-operations/system-requirements/)).

The rest of the field confirms the pattern. GitLab Free lacks epics, roadmaps, iterations and custom fields ([GitLab](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/group/epics/); [GitLab](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/work_items/custom_fields/)), and its Jira importer brings over little more than titles, descriptions and labels ([GitLab](https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/import/jira/)). Redmine 7.0 remains the benchmark for free configurability and now adds webhooks ([Redmine](https://www.redmine.org/news)). Tuleap says both its editions are fully open source and differ only in support and production-hardened builds ([Tuleap](https://docs.tuleap.com/user-guide/intro.html)), and the commercial YouTrack gives every feature to every tier, free up to 10 users and including a helpdesk with three free agents ([JetBrains](https://www.jetbrains.com/youtrack/features/); [JetBrains](https://blog.jetbrains.com/youtrack/2025/06/new-youtrack-prices-starting-from-october-2025/)). The research found no widely adopted open-source JQL grammar or JQL-to-SQL compiler; Atlassian's own ANTLR-based parser package is published for internal development ([Atlassian](https://atlaskit.atlassian.com/packages/jql/jql-parser)).

The category's failures supply the remaining design constraints. Huly's hosted service shut down because hosting was no longer funded, and its repository is frozen ([Huly](https://github.com/hcengineering/platform)); Mattermost put Boards into maintenance mode ([Mattermost](https://support.mattermost.com/hc/en-us/articles/19614000831252-Boards-Plugin-in-Maintenance-Mode)); Taiga's private, from-scratch rewrite stalled ([Taiga](https://community.taiga.io/t/important-update-on-taiganext-and-kaleidos/171)); and Kanboard is in maintenance mode ([Kanboard](https://github.com/kanboard/kanboard)). Planka moved to a non-OSI licence and removed SSO from its community edition in August 2026 ([Planka](https://github.com/plankanban/planka/blob/master/LICENSE.md); [daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/self-host-weekly-14-august-2026--txxhhkqv3)), and Mattermost v11 cut the free Team Edition's user limit from 1,000 to 250 and drew a community backlash ([Mattermost forum](https://forum.mattermost.com/t/mattermost-v11-changes-in-free-offerings/25126)). Plane's $799 lifetime licence earned several hundred thousand dollars, but under 5% of buyers upgraded and support load ballooned, so it was sunset ([Plane](https://plane.so/blog/lessons-in-sustainability-from-scaling-in-open-source)).

Commercial tools show what "best" looks like. Linear's speed comes from a sync engine built first: each client keeps a local IndexedDB copy and applies changes optimistically, while the server sends ordered deltas keyed by a single monotonic sync ID, resolves conflicts as last-write-wins without CRDTs, and limits each client's data through permission-scoped sync groups ([reverse-linear-sync-engine](https://github.com/wzhudev/reverse-linear-sync-engine)). Its Triage Intelligence suggests team, assignee, labels and duplicates with visible reasoning and a per-property switch to show, hide or auto-apply ([Linear](https://linear.app/docs/triage-intelligence)), and its agent API treats agents as non-billable delegates while a human stays the assignee ([Linear](https://linear.app/developers/agents)). Height, which pushed autonomous AI hard, shut down on 24 September 2025, and one reviewer blames unwanted AI automation ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/3/height-project-management-tool-to-shut-down-by-september-2025/); [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/height-app-alternative)). Azure DevOps exposes daily work-item snapshots through OData for BI ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/devops/report/powerbi/what-is-analytics?view=azure-devops)), GitHub lets users assign issues to Copilot, Claude or Codex ([GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-02-04-claude-and-codex-are-now-available-in-public-preview-on-github/)), and most suites now meter AI in credits ([ClickUp](https://clickup.com/pricing); [Asana](https://asana.com/pricing)). **[Estimate]** The uncontested position is the intersection nobody occupies: Linear-grade speed, Jira-grade configurability including per-state permissions and time tracking, OpenProject-grade planning depth, a JQL-compatible query language and a free, lossless Jira importer, all without an enterprise paywall.

## Full parity is about 700,000 lines of code; the exit core about 100,000

The best cost evidence comes from existing codebases. The research team's line count of public GitHub source measured Plane at about **346,500 lines** of non-test application code built since November 2022, and OpenProject at about **741,600 lines of application code plus 683,900 lines of tests** accumulated since 2012 ([GitHub](https://github.com/makeplane/plane); [GitHub](https://github.com/opf/openproject)). Plane reached that scale on a disclosed $4M seed, with third-party headcount estimates of 77–93 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/28/plane-takes-on-jira-with-open-source-project-management-tools-for-software-teams/); [BuiltIn LA](https://www.builtinla.com/company/plane); [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/startups/plane)); OpenProject had about 45 employees at its ten-year mark ([OpenProject](https://www.openproject.org/blog/employee-interview-birthe-lindenthal)); and Linear, the benchmark for polish, had about 50 people four years in and completed SOC 2 Type I about 16 months after launch ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/series-b); [Linear](https://linear.app/changelog/2021-10-21-soc-2)). Basic COCOMO applied to those measured sizes gives **93–175 person-years** for a Plane-scale product and **206–410** for OpenProject scale, broadly matching the observed team sizes (the research team's calculation from the repositories above). The table below extends the same model to this project's scope.

| Scope **[Estimate]** | Application code | Human-equivalent effort, COCOMO (organic to semi-detached) | Nominal schedule for a human team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exit core | ~100K lines | ~25 person-years (organic) | ~22 months |
| All-features product, base case | ~700K lines | 194–384 person-years | ~48 months |
| All-features product, scope creep | ~1M lines | 283–573 person-years | ~55 months |

**[Estimate]** These figures measure scope, not staffing. For a human team the binding constraint would be schedule: building the ~100,000-line exit core in 15 months runs at about 70% of its nominal 22-month schedule, near the roughly 75% compression floor that COCOMO II planning treats as a practical limit. For an AI-built project the constraint moves to verification: every feature needs tests, fixtures and benchmarks that prove parity, which is why the separate build plan is organised around automated exit gates.

Two hidden costs deserve an explicit budget. OpenProject carries almost one line of test per line of application code and Redmine exactly one ([GitHub](https://github.com/opf/openproject); [GitHub](https://github.com/redmine/redmine)), against a measured 4% for Plane, and regulated buyers will expect the former. Locale files run to 884,000 lines in OpenProject against 140,000 in Plane (same measurement), so translation has to be a community workflow from day one.

Jira's own internals show what to avoid. Jira stores custom-field values in a generic `customfieldvalue` table, and Atlassian's scale tests found custom-field count was the largest of eight latency drivers, with instances carrying **2,800 fields markedly slower than those with 1,400** ([Atlassian](https://confluence.atlassian.com/enterprise/managing-custom-fields-in-jira-effectively-945523781.html)). The Cloud monolith runs one vertically scaled Postgres database per customer, and some customers hit the largest available RDS instance ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/how-we-build/reducing-jql-database-load-with-caches)). Atlassian's December 2025 platform rebuild moved issues into a document store, search into a separate OpenSearch index and permission checks into a central authorization service with a **10 ms target**, cutting issue-navigator p99 from about **4 s to about 300 ms** and designing for up to a billion issues per tenant ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/how-we-build/how-we-unlocked-performance-at-scale-with-jira-platform)); on Data Center, OpenSearch cut P95 latency from 813 to 500 ms and matched five-node performance on three nodes ([Atlassian](https://confluence.atlassian.com/enterprise/opensearch-for-jira-data-center-1680483103.html)). **[Recommendation]** Start where Atlassian is retrofitting: typed field storage, precomputed permissions, a search layer that can move out of the main database, and bounded per-item history.

Self-hosting footprint is a selling point in its own right. Plane needs at least 2 vCPU and 4 GB of RAM ([Plane](https://developers.plane.so/self-hosting/methods/install-methods-commercial/docker-compose)), Huly needs 8–16 GB and can stop responding on smaller servers ([Huly](https://github.com/hcengineering/huly-selfhost)), and Sentry self-hosted wants 16 GB plus swap across about 20 containers ([Bugsink](https://www.bugsink.com/blog/why-i-gave-up-on-self-hosted-sentry/)). **[Recommendation]** Target 2–4 vCPU and 4–8 GB with PostgreSQL as the only required stateful service, which matters most to air-gapped and sovereign buyers on constrained VMs.

The importer is both feasible and differentiating. Atlassian's own migration assistant leaves behind dashboards, cross-project boards and filters, filter subscriptions, webhooks and global permissions ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/migration/docs/what-gets-migrated-with-the-jira-cloud-migration-assistant/)). Jira Cloud API traffic made with a customer's own API token is exempt from the new points quotas and only burst-limited ([Atlassian](https://developer.atlassian.com/cloud/jira/platform/rate-limiting)), and Data Center backups arrive as `entities.xml` and `activeobjects.xml` plus attachments, with Atlassian shipping a cleaner because control characters break imports ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/jira/kb/unable-to-find-jira-backup-entitiesxml-inside-of-zip-file-error/)). The research team estimates a 1M-issue Cloud import at about 5M API calls, or 1–3 days of wall-clock time, while DC XML import avoids the API entirely. **[Recommendation]** Read DC backups directly, pull Cloud data with the customer's token, keep Atlassian Document Format originals beside converted rich text (open converters such as marklas already exist ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/marklas/))), and import everything the official tool drops.

**[Recommendation]** Forking is the main alternative to a greenfield build, and it loses on the pieces that matter most. Plane's AGPL edition would save UI work, but its community data model has no custom-property table ([GitHub](https://github.com/makeplane/plane/blob/preview/apps/api/plane/db/models/issue.py)), so typed fields with contexts, schemes, workflows and JQL would still be built from scratch on top of a fast-diverging upstream. OpenProject brings depth and a public-sector channel but also the system-wide, generic-table custom fields that slowed Jira. A greenfield core that reuses proven components is the better path: Tiptap, Yjs and Hocuspocus for collaborative text, as Plane uses them ([GitHub](https://github.com/makeplane/plane/blob/preview/apps/live/package.json)); OpenFGA for authorization, a CNCF Incubating project since October 2025 ([CNCF](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2025/11/11/openfga-becomes-a-cncf-incubating-project/)); and Extism for sandboxed WASM plugins ([Extism](https://extism.org)).

## Contracts and trademarks, not API copyright, set the legal perimeter

The sharpest legal constraints are contractual. Atlassian's Customer Agreement, effective 1 October 2026, bars customers from using the products to "develop a similar or competing product or service", and also bars reverse engineering, seeking non-public APIs and creating derivative works, while confirming that customers own their data ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/legal/atlassian-customer-agreement)). The Developer Terms of 1 December 2025 bar building a competitive product on the Atlassian platform, publishing Marketplace apps that substantially replicate Atlassian features, and accessing the platform for competitive analysis or publishing benchmarks ([Atlassian](https://developer.atlassian.com/platform/marketplace/atlassian-developer-terms/)). **[Recommendation]** No one building the product, human or AI agent, should do competitive development under Atlassian accounts bound by those terms, and the importer should ship inside the product and be run by customers with their own tokens, never as a Marketplace app.

Copyright is the smaller risk. In *Google v. Oracle* (2021) the US Supreme Court held that reimplementing Java's declaring code was fair use ([Jones Day](https://jonesday.com/de/insights/2021/04/supreme-court-google-wins-copyright-battle)), and in *SAS v. World Programming* (2012) the CJEU held that functionality, programming languages and data formats are not protected — but also that manuals are, and that the selection and arrangement of keywords and syntax can be ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_Institute_Inc_v_World_Programming_Ltd)). **[Recommendation]** Write all documentation independently, never decompile Jira jars, and offer JQL and the Jira-shaped REST API as a separate compatibility module over a native query language and API, so any residual EU claim about keyword selection touches only an optional layer.

Trademark and patent exposure is manageable. Atlassian's guidelines allow referential phrases such as "for Jira" or "compatible with Jira" but ban its marks in product names and domains ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/legal/trademark)). Atlassian holds narrow issue-tracking patents, such as US 12032954 on integration with source-control events, plus patents on chat-to-issue creation ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12032954); [Justia](https://patents.justia.com/assignee/atlassian-pty-ltd?page=7)); its patent litigation record is defensive, with one 2025 suit against it dismissed after 23 days ([PatSnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/litigation/tg-2006-holdings-v-atlassian-us8583514b2-patent-dismissed-patsnap/)), and the research found no case of Atlassian suing a clone. **[Recommendation]** Run a freedom-to-operate review before building source-control and AI chat-to-issue features, and join a defensive patent pool.

The license should stay open source in the OSI sense. AGPL-3.0 matches Plane and Vikunja and deters SaaS free-riding, whereas the Functional Source License converts to Apache or MIT only after two years and is not OSI-approved ([FSL](https://fsl.software/)), which conflicts with the open-source requirements of the European public buyers behind openDesk. GitLab replaced its CLA with the lighter Developer Certificate of Origin in 2017 because the CLA was deterring contributors ([GitLab](https://about.gitlab.com/blog/gitlab-switches-to-dco-license/)).

The business model has to fit the brief that every premium feature ships free. Two existence proofs support it: Tuleap's paid edition differs only in support and hardened builds ([Tuleap](https://docs.tuleap.com/user-guide/intro.html)), and YouTrack sells every feature in every tier ([JetBrains](https://www.jetbrains.com/youtrack/features/)). The open-core alternative charges for identity and governance features instead, but the backlash at Plane, Planka and Mattermost shows the reputational cost of clawing such features back later. **[Recommendation]** Sell assurance rather than features: managed hosting; an enterprise subscription covering long-term-support builds, signed air-gap bundles, response SLAs, early security advisories, compliance-mapping packs and indemnity; fixed-fee DC exit migrations; and partner certification.

**[Estimate]** Without a salaried team, the running costs are compute, AI usage, continuous integration, hosting for any public demo, release-signing infrastructure and third-party audits. Revenue from hosting, support or migrations matters only if the project offers those services. Linear shows a well-run tracker can become self-funding, but it did so as proprietary SaaS after a $4.2M seed and a $13M Series A ([Salestools](https://salestools.io/en/blog/linear-raises-35m-series-b); [Linear](https://linear.app/now/series-b)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what the project is about. The scarce asset is not another board UI — the open-source field already has more than twenty — but the compatibility layer: an importer that carries more than Atlassian's own migration assistant, a query engine that runs customers' saved JQL filters unchanged, and a Jira-shaped REST shim that keeps their scripts and integrations alive. That layer turns Atlassian's lock-in into the remake's acquisition channel, and it has an expiry date, because Data Center goes read-only in March 2029. Breadth can follow, since organizations that have just finished one migration rarely start another.

"Every premium feature free" is also cheap in code and expensive in assurance. SSO, SCIM, audit, approvals and cross-team planning are ordinary engineering, but enterprises pay for long-term support, signed air-gap bundles, compliance evidence and someone to call at 3 a.m., so any commercial offering around the remake should sell those. Finally, Atlassian's own 2025–2026 rebuild is a free architecture review: a document-style issue store, a separate search index, a central authorization service and enforced configuration limits are where a newcomer should start, not where it should end up after a decade.
